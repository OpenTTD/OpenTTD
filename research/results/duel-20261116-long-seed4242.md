# A long duel in one game: planned bot vs unplanned bot (seed 4242, 1950-01 to 1970-11)

Started as `tools/research/mcp-duel --months 120 --seed 4242` with the complete bot (bridge
repository 34bd520: turns as the game fits them, level crossings, bridges, tunnels, up to four
freight lines as money allows, station joins). Company A (#1) is PrismaticAI from the planner's
portfolio; company B (#2) is the same AI without a plan.

**Stopped by hand at 1970-11, almost 21 game years, and written up from the duel's own log**
(`duel-20261116-long-seed4242.log`, its monthly lines), because the script writes its result
file only at the end. The run used mcp-duel's old month counter, which counted game.month events
in the panel's capped event buffer and fell behind the game: its "month 80" is dated 1970-11. The
counter now comes from the game's date (fixed the same day), so the log's month labels are wrong

## Reading this result

A led from 1952 on and kept about ten times B's value for two decades (10.7 to 1 at the end).
Company value is money minus loan plus vehicles, so most of A's value is accumulated cash: the
bot builds at most four freight lines, and once they are built it has nothing to spend on. A also
lost trains over time (five in 1952, three by 1970). The fleet manager sells a vehicle that loses
money two years running, and an ageing train is not replaced. What a longer game needs next:
replacing old trains, more lines once the four are built, and other cargo.

The baseline is still the same bot without a plan. A duel against a strong AI (AdmiralAI or
SimpleAI, `mcp-duel --opponent`) waits for those AIs to be downloaded.
